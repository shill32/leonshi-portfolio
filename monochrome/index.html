Chapter 01

Product investment / Executive review

A recurring view of where product investment returns value.

The problem

Product investment decisions draw on experiments, operating context, and assumptions that can be hard to compare. A one-time analysis is not enough when priorities and evidence keep changing.

What I built

I own the analysis used in Audible’s recurring product return-on-investment and experimentation review. It gives leaders a consistent view of tradeoffs across product areas and evidence from prior experiments.

How it is used

The work supports a recurring review with the Chief Product & Analytics Officer. Teams can return to the same evidence, revisit assumptions, and compare allocation choices in an established decision forum.

Selected technical details

  • Combines experimentation reads with product investment assumptions.
  • Preserves the reasoning behind comparisons so it can be reviewed again.
  • Treats the executive review as a durable operating system, not a presentation artifact.

Chapter 02

Synthetic research / Agent-based evaluation

Synthetic experiments checked against experiments that already ran.

The problem

Generated user research can sound convincing without predicting how people respond to a real product change. The useful question is not whether an agent writes a plausible explanation. It is whether the system’s read holds up against observed behavior.

What I built

I built an agent-based synthetic experimentation system that simulates responses from diverse participants and aggregates the resulting effects. The system then grades those reads against prior web experiments.

How it is used

The comparison reveals where synthetic findings agree with historical experiment results and where they fail. That makes the system useful as an evaluated research method rather than an ungrounded source of model prose.

Selected technical details

  • Generates participant-level responses across a varied synthetic cohort.
  • Aggregates responses into an experiment-level read.
  • Compares that read with historical results and exposes errors for inspection.

Chapter 03

Options research / Live product

Cronos makes an options strategy reproducible from definition to replay.

The problem

Options research has many linked decisions: strategy rules, contract selection, historical data, and portfolio behavior. When those steps are disconnected, it is difficult to inspect why a result changed or reproduce the path to it.

What I built

I built Cronos end to end as a live options research product. The work includes data pipelines, strategy definition, contract resolution, historical replay, the product interface, authentication, billing, and deployment.

How it is used

A researcher can define a strategy, resolve the relevant contracts, and replay a portfolio against historical data. The sequence keeps research decisions inspectable from the first rule through the resulting portfolio path.

Selected technical details

  • Connects strategy definition to contract resolution.
  • Replays portfolio behavior against historical market data.
  • Runs as a production product with account, billing, and deployment systems.

Chapter 04

Computational physics / Research prototype

A computational physics prototype for screening large parameter spaces.

The problem

Scientific parameter spaces can be too large to explore by hand, while language models cannot verify whether their own proposals satisfy the physics. Search and evaluation need separate responsibilities.

What I built

I built a computational physics prototype that proposes candidate theories and moves them through numerical evaluation. Current work searches topological-material and dark-matter parameter spaces.

How it is used

The prototype broadens the candidate set, runs numerical sweeps, and retains proposals that pass deterministic checks. It supports exploration without asking a generative model to judge its own output.

Selected technical details

  • Separates candidate generation from numerical evaluation.
  • Searches parameter spaces with repeatable sweeps.
  • Uses deterministic kernels as the acceptance boundary.
